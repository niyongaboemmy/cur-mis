# CUR-MIS — University Management Information System
### A Plain-Language Overview

**Prepared for:** University leadership, department heads, staff, and non-technical stakeholders
**Last updated:** August 2026

---

## 1. What This System Is

CUR-MIS is the single online platform that runs the day-to-day business of the Catholic University of Rwanda. It replaces the scattered mix of paper forms, spreadsheets, and separate office systems that a university typically accumulates over time.

In one sentence: **it follows a person from the moment they first apply to the university, through their studies, to the day they graduate — and it manages the staff, money, and paperwork that make that journey possible.**

Everyone who deals with the university has a place in it:

- **Prospective students** apply online and track their application.
- **Students** register for courses, see their marks, pay fees, and request documents.
- **Lecturers** manage their classes, record attendance, and enter marks.
- **Administrative staff** handle admissions, records, finance, and human resources.
- **Leadership** sees the numbers that show how the institution is performing.

The platform is organised around four broad areas of university life:

## 3. The Student Journey

### 3.1 Admissions — Getting In

A person who wants to study at the university starts on a **public application page**. No account or office visit is needed to begin.

The process works like this:

1. **Apply online.** The applicant fills in personal details, previous education, and the programme they want. They choose a campus, a study mode, and an intake period.
2. **Upload documents.** Certificates, transcripts, identification, and any other required paperwork are uploaded directly. The system knows which documents each programme requires.
3. **Track progress.** The applicant gets a reference and can check their status at any time without contacting the office.
4. **Verification.** Admissions staff review each application and each uploaded document one at a time, approving them or sending them back with a comment explaining what needs fixing.
5. **Ranking.** The system can automatically score and rank applicants against published criteria, producing a **merit list** — a fair, visible ordering of who qualifies.
6. **Offers.** Successful applicants receive an official admission letter, generated automatically as a PDF and emailed to them.
7. **Enrolment.** Accepting an offer turns the applicant into a registered student, carrying their details forward — nothing is re-typed.

Admissions staff also manage the rules behind all of this: which programmes are open, what each one requires, which document types are accepted, and which intake periods are running. Staff can also admit a student manually when circumstances call for it, and there is dedicated handling for **international students**, including visa record tracking.

### 3.2 Academic Records — The Structure of Study

The system holds the university's academic skeleton: **schools, faculties, departments, programmes, degrees, levels, campuses, academic years and terms**. Everything else hangs off this structure.

Within it sits the **module (course) catalogue** — the individual subjects students take, each with its credits, level, prerequisites, and the programmes it belongs to.

### 3.3 Modules and Registration — Choosing Courses

Each term, modules are **offered**, **scheduled** into rooms and time slots, and **assigned** to lecturers. Students then register for the modules they will take. Administrators can register students in bulk or handle individual cases, and students can see their own registrations at any time.

The system checks that students meet prerequisites and stay within the rules of their programme.

### 3.4 Teaching and Attendance

Lecturers have their own working area of the platform showing their courses, the students in each, their teaching calendar, and their upcoming exams.

**Attendance** is recorded per class session. Registers can be locked once finalised so records cannot be quietly changed afterwards.

### 3.5 Assessment and Results

Marks are entered by lecturers against a published **grading scale**, so a raw score always converts to a grade the same way across the institution.

Beyond simple mark entry, the system supports the full examination cycle:

- **Exam scheduling** — timetabling exams into rooms and sessions, with attendance taken at the exam itself.
- **Deliberation** — the formal meeting where a board reviews results and decides outcomes, supported by on-screen mark sheets.
- **Revaluation** — a student's formal request to have a mark reviewed, tracked from request to decision.
- **Superseded marks** — when a mark is corrected or a resit is taken, the old figure is kept but clearly marked as replaced, so the history stays honest.

### 3.6 Graduation and Official Documents

Toward the end of a student's studies, the system:

- **Audits** each candidate against the requirements of their programme and flags anyone falling short.
- **Classifies** the degree (for example, the class of honours) from the accumulated results.
- Manages **clearance** — confirming the student owes nothing to the finance office, library, or other departments before graduating.
- Produces the **graduand list** and a **graduation booklet**.

It also issues official paperwork: **transcripts**, **academic certificates**, **student ID cards**, registration forms, and other institutional documents — generated from live data, so they always match the record.

There is a **public verification page** where an employer or another institution can confirm that a document or a student is genuine, without needing to phone the registry.

## 4. The Institution's People — Human Resources

The HR side of the platform mirrors the student side, for staff.

- **Staff records** — personal details, position, department, employment history, and qualifications.
- **Staff attendance** — daily presence tracking.
- **Payroll** — salary calculation with allowances and deductions, payslips staff can view themselves, and a record of payments made. Payroll rules and rates are configurable rather than hard-coded.
- **Leave** — staff request leave, managers approve or decline it, and the system tracks how much leave each person has left across different leave types.
- **Appraisals** — structured performance reviews.
- **HR documents** — contracts and other staff paperwork, stored centrally.

## 5. The Institution's Money — Finance

Finance is one of the largest parts of the platform, covering both income and expenditure.

### 5.1 What Students Owe

- **Fee structures** define what each programme, level, campus, and student category costs. There are separate arrangements for postgraduate and international students, and a per-credit charging model where that applies.
- **Invoices** are generated for students, in bulk at the start of a term or individually.
- **Bursaries and sponsors** reduce what a student personally owes; sponsoring organisations are tracked as their own records.
- **Fines** cover penalties such as late payment or breaches of regulations.
- **Payment calendars** publish the deadlines by which instalments must be paid.
- **Overdue alerts** flag students who have fallen behind.

### 5.2 Money Coming In

Students pay through several channels, all of which land in the same place:

- **Online payments** through the integrated mobile and bank payment gateway, where a student pays and the system records it automatically.
- **Bank and manual payments**, recorded by finance staff and, where required, passed through an **approval step** before they count.
- **Refunds**, where money must go back to the student.

Each payment produces a **receipt**, and students can see their own balance and payment history at any time.

A **reconciliation** function compares what the system believes was paid against what the payment provider and bank actually report, so discrepancies surface quickly rather than at year end.

### 5.3 Money Going Out

- **Budget plans** set out what each department expects to spend.
- **Expenses** are recorded against categories and against those budgets.
- **Budget execution** shows planned versus actual spending as the year progresses.

### 5.4 Reporting

Finance leadership gets an overview dashboard, revenue reports, account balances, and a student-by-student billing view — the numbers needed to answer "where do we stand?" without assembling a spreadsheet.

## 6. Operations and Everyday Working

### 6.1 Service Requests — The Digital Front Desk

Rather than students queuing at different offices for different things, the platform runs a **service catalogue**: a published list of the services the university offers (a transcript, a proof-of-enrolment letter, a name correction, and so on).

Each service defines:

- who may request it,
- what documents must be attached,
- what it costs, if anything,
- and **which offices must approve it, in what order**.

A request then moves through those approval stages automatically, visibly, with each approver seeing only the queue that belongs to them. Requesters — including members of the public, who can submit and track a request without an account — can see exactly where their request is sitting and why.

Staff get dashboards and reports showing volumes, bottlenecks, and turnaround times.

### 6.2 Communication

- **Announcements** — official notices published to the relevant audience.
- **Messages** — direct conversations between users of the platform.
- **Forums** — threaded discussion areas organised by category, for less formal exchange.
- **Email** — used throughout for admission letters, notifications, and confirmations.
- **AI assistant** — a built-in helper that answers users' questions about how to use the platform and where to find things.

### 6.3 Campus Security — Gate Management

Entry and exit at campus gates are logged, giving a record of movement on and off the premises.

### 6.4 Search and Navigation

A single search reaches across students, staff, and records, so users can find what they need without knowing which office it belongs to. Menus adapt to each user — people only see what they are entitled to use.

## 7. Who Sees What — Access and Control

Access is not all-or-nothing. The system is built around **roles** and **permissions**.

- A **permission** is a single specific right, such as "record marks" or "approve payments." There are dozens of them, grouped into twelve areas: Administration, System Settings, Academic Registry, Modules Management, Examinations, Attendance, Admissions, HR Management, Finance, Student Clearance, External Portals, and Applicant Self-Service.
- A **role** is a bundle of those permissions matching a job — for example *registrar*, *finance officer*, *HR manager*, *lecturer*, *student*, or *applicant*.
- Each user holds a role, and administrators can adjust what any role may do without a developer being involved.

Users can also be tied to a specific **campus**, so their view is limited to the site they work at.

Logging in is protected by a **one-time code sent to the user**, in addition to their password, and there is a self-service password recovery route.

## 8. Accountability — The Audit Trail

Significant actions across the platform are recorded: who did it, what changed, and when. Administrators can review this history through a dedicated screen.

This matters for three reasons: disputes can be settled with evidence, mistakes can be traced to their source, and the institution can demonstrate to auditors and regulators that its records are controlled.

## 9. How It Fits Together, Practically

The platform has two halves that work as one product:

- **What users see** — a modern web interface that works on desktop and mobile, with light and dark viewing modes. Everything is reached through a browser; nothing is installed on a user's machine.
- **What runs behind it** — the engine that holds the data, enforces the rules, and generates the documents.

Alongside these sit two supporting pieces: a **document store** that holds uploaded and generated files, and a **payment connection** that links the university to its mobile-money and banking provider.

The system is hosted online and reached through a normal web address. Updates to the software, changes to the database structure, and the loading of reference data are handled as three separate, controlled steps — so a routine update to the interface carries no risk to stored records.

## 10. What the Institution Gets Out of It

**One version of the truth.** A student's programme, marks, fee balance, and documents all come from the same place. The registry and the finance office are never working from different figures.

**Work that moves by itself.** Applications, approvals, and service requests advance through defined stages without anyone carrying paper between offices.

**Visibility.** Applicants, students, and staff can answer their own questions — "where is my application?", "what do I owe?", "has my request been approved?" — which removes a large volume of routine enquiries from front-desk staff.

**Documents that are always correct.** Letters, transcripts, receipts, and certificates are generated from live records, so they cannot drift out of date, and they can be verified by outsiders.

**Control.** Permissions are granular, access is tied to the job, and every meaningful action leaves a trace.

**Evidence for decisions.** Admissions, academic performance, finance, and service delivery each produce reporting that leadership can act on.
